The Ponte Vittorio Emanuele I is a bridge that crosses the river Po, at the eastern area of the center of Turin.

It is, in fact, the main city bridge, since it combines an important part of the historic center (Piazza Vittorio Veneto) with Piazza Gran Madre di Dio, on the right bank of the Po (Borgo Po [district[1]).

It is often called by the Turinese simply as the bridge of the Gran Madre, bridge of Piazza Vittorio or bridge of the Murazzi.

History and description

[edit | edit source]

During the French occupation of Turin at the beginning of the 19th century, on the direct order of Napoleon it was decided to build a solid and massive bridge, replacing the previous stone bridge, now unstable.[ 2] The previous bridge, in fact, dates back to 1404, designed in stone by Antonio Becchio da Villanova, with 12 pillars, about 10 meters wide, and commissioned by Pope Martin V. The structure suffered various damage over time, one for all a significant flood of the river Po on November 3, 1706, which destroyed some arches, then replaced by precarious wooden structures.[ 3]].

Even before, or in Roman times and then in the Middle Ages, there were various versions of temporary bridges, of access to the Roman castrum of Augusta Taurinorum towards the Porta Fibellona (Piazza Castello) / Via Po - and called the Porta di Po - built entirely of wood, of which a first certain documentation is of a draws station commissioned by Bishop Landolfo in 1037. For the massive passage of goods, however, the traditional river toll ferry[4] was often preferred[4].

The construction of the current Napoleonic bridge involved the demolition of a warehouse building and the church of Saints Mark and Leonardo, located in the current point between Piazza Vittorio Veneto corner Via Bonafus. The latter, was erected by the Barrachi in 1333, then already shot down in 1351 because it was too sensitive a military target near the bridge; it was then rebuilt by the Vittone in 1740, and again torn down in 1811 to give space to the access of the new bridge[[5].

The laying of the foundation stone of the bridge took place in November 1810, in the presence of Prince Camillo Borghese, husband of Paolina Bonaparte and then Napoleonic Governor in PiemontePiedmont: 88 coins and medals commemorating the Napoleonic countryside, as well as a silver meter, were placed in the central pillar of the bridge[.[1] The work was carried out by the French engineer Charles Mallet and the Piedmontese Pellegrini, designed by Claude La Ramée Pertinchamp, with five arches, 150 meters long and 12.9 meters wide, then finished in 1813. A year later, with the end of the French occupation and with the return of the Savoy to the city, it was proposed to bring it down, perceived by many as the symbol of the past occupation, but King Vittorio Emanuele I opposed the idea and, both the bridge and the great Piazza Vittorio (formerly Piazza d'Armi), were both titled to him. In the same year of his return (1814), the construction of the square and the church of the Great Mother of God was also decided, from the opposite side of the bridge, in the Borgo Po district, the latter, built only in 1831.

Main article: Church of the Great Mother of God (Turin) and Borgo Po.

The bridge suffered virtually no changes from its opening, resisting the various floods. Only work was carried out in 1876, to allow the passage of the tram, which also involved the replacement of the old stone parapets with the current cast iron parapets.
